1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: The directive defines statutory interest for late payment as "simple interest
   for late payment at a rate which is equal to the sum of the reference rate and at
   least eight percentage points." The reference rate is the interest rate applied by the
   European Central Bank to its most recent main refinancing operations (or the marginal
   rate from variable-rate tender procedures).

   *Source 1, Article 2(6) and Article 2(7)(a).*

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: No, that is not correct. The directive sets a **minimum** fixed compensation
   sum of EUR 40 — not a maximum of EUR 25. The directive states that "the creditor is
   entitled to obtain from the debtor, as a minimum, a fixed sum of EUR 40" and that
   this sum is payable without the necessity of a reminder. The creditor may also claim
   additional reasonable recovery costs beyond that EUR 40.

   *Source 1, Article 6(1) and Article 6(2). Source 2 (owner's note) — the owner's
   belief that "the EU maximum … is 25 EUR" is not supported by the directive.*

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No. The directive explicitly states that the creditor is entitled to interest
   for late payment "without the necessity of a reminder" once the payment period has
   expired and the creditor has fulfilled its obligations. Similarly, the fixed
   compensation sum of EUR 40 is "payable without the necessity of a reminder."
   Therefore, sending a reminder is not a prerequisite under the directive for claiming
   statutory interest or the fixed compensation.

   *Source 1, Article 3(1) and Article 6(2).*

   Note: The owner's note (Source 2) states "I think we can only do that after we have
   sent them a reminder." This belief is contradicted by the directive.

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: Not in the sources. The directive explains how the reference rate is defined
   (the ECB's main refinancing operations rate) and states that the rate applicable for
   each semester is the rate in force on 1 January or 1 July of that year (Source 1,
   Article 3(2)), but neither source file provides the current numerical ECB reference
   rate. To obtain it, the owner would need to consult the ECB's official publications
   or a current reliable financial data source.
